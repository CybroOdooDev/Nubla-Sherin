1.  Executive Summary
This module is a full incident reporting and risk register system in the mould of Datix / RLDatix — the de-facto standard in UK healthcare — built natively on Odoo. It is deliberately STANDALONE: it has no dependency on the NHS Trust Management suite, so it can be sold to the entire CQC-registered market (~30,000 organisations in England alone), not just NHS Trusts. A thin auto-installing bridge module links it into the trust hierarchy when both products are present.
1.1  What the module delivers
Unlimited-reporter incident capture: a public web form (no Odoo login, optionally anonymous) modelled on Datix's famous DIF1 form — the feature that made Datix ubiquitous in the NHS.
A handler workspace (the 'DIF2' equivalent): triage queue, validation, NPSA harm grading, categorisation, person-affected records, investigation assignment.
PSIRF-aligned proportionate investigation: SWARM huddle, After Action Review, MDT review, and full Patient Safety Incident Investigation (PSII) response types.
Statutory compliance engines: Duty of Candour tracker with 10-working-day clock, RIDDOR determination and HSE report log, CQC statutory notification tracker, LFPSE-aligned event taxonomy and export.
A board-grade Risk Register: 5×5 NPSA-style scoring, inherent/current/target ratings, controls and assurances, review cycles, escalation between register tiers, heatmap reporting.
CAPA action management linked to both incidents and risks, with owners, due dates, evidence and overdue escalation.
Provider-type adaptation: the same module reconfigures terminology, categories and notification rules for NHS trusts, GP practices/PCNs, care homes, domiciliary care, hospices, independent hospitals, pharmacies and dental practices.
1.2  Commercial thesis
RLDatix pricing (typically five to six figures per year for a trust, with per-module licensing and heavy implementation consultancy) has left two huge segments completely unserved: primary care (~6,300 GP practices, ~1,250 PCNs) and adult social care (~15,000+ CQC-registered providers). These organisations have identical statutory duties — incident recording, RIDDOR, CQC notifications, Duty of Candour, risk management evidence for inspection — but run on paper and spreadsheets. An Odoo-priced product that does 90% of Datix at 5% of the cost wins this market on price alone, while the NHS-trust bridge keeps the premium suite story intact.
2.  Study — How Datix / RLDatix Works
This section records the functional study of Datix that drives the design. Feature names below follow Datix terminology so a buyer migrating from Datix recognises the workflow immediately.
2.1  Product background
Datix Ltd founded in the UK in 1986; merged with Canada's RL Solutions in 2018 to form RLDatix.
Two product generations are in the field: legacy on-premise 'Datix Web' (CIMS — Claims & Incident Management System) and the cloud platform DatixCloudIQ (DCIQ).
Historically used by the large majority of NHS trusts; also sold to ambulance services, ICBs and some private hospital groups.
Licensed per module (Incidents, Risk, Complaints, Claims, Safety Alerts, Mortality, Safeguarding) — a key cost driver we deliberately avoid by shipping incidents+risk together.
2.2  The DIF1 / DIF2 pattern (incident capture)
Datix's defining design is the split between the reporter form and the handler record:
Form
Who uses it
Characteristics
DIF1
ANY member of staff — no licence, no login on most deployments. Optionally anonymous.
Short capture form: date/time, location picker, category picker, description, persons affected, immediate action taken, reporter details (optional). Designed to take < 5 minutes.
DIF2
Trained handlers / risk team (licensed users).
The full management record opened from the DIF1 submission: validation/rejection, re-grading, NPSA harm level, approval status chain, investigation, linked records, contacts, documents, feedback to reporter.
Design implication: our module MUST have a public capture form decoupled from licensed users, a triage queue, and a reporter-feedback loop ('you said, we did' is a CQC well-led inspection theme).

2.4  Harm grading (NPSA scale used by Datix)
Grade
Definition
No harm
Incident occurred but no harm resulted (includes prevented incidents / near misses in some configurations).
Low harm
Minimal harm — required extra observation or minor treatment.
Moderate harm
Significant but not permanent harm; moderate increase in treatment. TRIGGERS Duty of Candour.
Severe harm
Permanent or long-term harm. Triggers Duty of Candour and usually a PSII-level response.
Death
Death caused or contributed to by the incident. Triggers Duty of Candour, PSII, and statutory notifications.
LFPSE additionally grades physical and psychological harm separately — our model stores both, plus the legacy single NPSA grade for migration compatibility.
2.5  Datix Risk Register module
One risk record per identified risk, written in cause → event → effect form ('IF [cause] THEN [event] RESULTING IN [effect]').
5×5 scoring matrix (consequence 1–5 × likelihood 1–5 = rating 1–25) — the NPSA 2008 matrix that virtually every NHS organisation uses.
Three scores per risk: inherent/gross (before controls), current/net (with existing controls), target/residual (after planned actions).
Controls in place + gaps in control; assurances (1st line: management, 2nd line: oversight/compliance, 3rd line: internal audit) + gaps in assurance — the 'three lines of defence' model boards expect.
Tiered registers: local/departmental → directorate/divisional → corporate risk register (CRR) → Board Assurance Framework (BAF). Risks escalate and de-escalate between tiers.
Review cycles driven by rating: extreme risks reviewed monthly, high quarterly, moderate six-monthly, low annually (typical policy).
Risk appetite statements per category; rating vs appetite drives escalation prompts.

2.7  Datix weaknesses this module exploits
Price: five-to-six-figure annual cost plus implementation consultancy — completely out of reach for primary care and social care.
Per-module licensing: risk register, complaints etc. all cost extra. We ship incidents + risk + actions in one module.
Dated UI: legacy Datix Web is a 2000s-era form interface; even DCIQ is form-heavy. Odoo's kanban/activity/chatter UX is a generational improvement.
Island system: Datix has no native link to HR, payroll, maintenance, procurement or document management. Inside Odoo, an incident can reference an hr.employee, raise a maintenance.request for a faulty bed, or attach to a quality audit — no integration project needed.
Reporting: Datix dashboards are rigid; deep analysis needs report-writer expertise. Odoo pivot/graph views give self-service analytics out of the box.
Configuration burden: Datix form/workflow changes are admin-intensive and often chargeable. Our provider-type presets configure the system in one click.
3.  Regulatory Landscape Encoded in the Module
Incident management in UK health and care is not optional record-keeping — it is a web of statutory duties. The module hard-codes the four frameworks below as first-class engines, because they are the reasons buyers MUST have a system.
3.1  PSIRF — Patient Safety Incident Response Framework
Replaced the Serious Incident Framework across NHS-funded care (national rollout completed autumn 2023).
Core shift: away from automatic RCA on every 'Serious Incident', towards PROPORTIONATE learning responses chosen from a toolkit.
Response types the module models: SWARM huddle (immediate, short), After Action Review (AAR), Multi-Disciplinary Team review (MDT), Patient Safety Incident Investigation (PSII — the deepest level), and 'no separate response' (learning captured in the record itself).
Organisations maintain a Patient Safety Incident Response Plan (PSIRP) defining which incident profiles get which response — our category model carries a default_response_level field to encode this.
Applies to NHS trusts AND any provider of NHS-funded care (including independent providers and some primary care) — another reason the standalone design is correct.
3.2  LFPSE — Learn from Patient Safety Events
The national NHS England recording service that replaced NRLS and StEIS.
Event taxonomy: four event types — Patient safety incident, Risk (near miss / hazard observed), Outcome (harm detected without a known incident), Good care (excellence worth learning from). Our incident_kind field mirrors this exactly.
Separate physical-harm and psychological-harm grading (none / low / moderate / severe / fatal).
Providers of NHS-funded care are expected to record compatible data and submit to LFPSE. v1 ships an LFPSE-aligned CSV/JSON export; live API submission is roadmap.
3.3  Duty of Candour (CQC Regulation 20)
Statutory duty on ALL CQC-registered providers (health AND social care) when a 'notifiable safety incident' occurs — broadly moderate harm or worse.
Requirements: tell the affected person face-to-face as soon as practicable, apologise, provide a written notification within 10 working days, share findings of any investigation.
The module ships a Duty of Candour sub-record with a working-day deadline clock, contact log, letter template, and evidence attachments — auto-created whenever harm grade reaches the trigger threshold.
Note: the precise 'notifiable safety incident' definition differs slightly between health bodies (Reg 20(8)) and other providers (Reg 20(9)) — the provider-type setting selects the correct definition text.
3.4  RIDDOR 2013 (HSE)
Applies to EVERY employer — trusts, GP practices, care homes alike — for work-related injuries to staff (and to non-workers injured by work activity).
Reportable: deaths, 'specified injuries' (fractures other than fingers/thumbs/toes, amputations, loss of sight, crush injuries, serious burns, etc.), over-7-day incapacitation, occupational diseases, dangerous occurrences.
Deadlines: deaths/specified injuries — report without delay (online) and within 10 days; over-7-day injuries — within 15 days.
The module ships a RIDDOR determination wizard (guided questions → reportable yes/no + category) and a report log holding the HSE submission reference.
3.5  CQC statutory notifications
All registered providers must notify CQC of: deaths of service users, serious injuries, allegations of abuse, applications/outcomes under Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS), events that stop the service running, and more (Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regs 16–18).
NHS trusts route most notifications via national systems; non-NHS providers (care homes, GPs, dentists, independent hospitals) submit directly to CQC — making this tracker MOST valuable to exactly the segments Datix ignores.
The module maps incident categories to notification types and tracks: required?, form type, submitted date, CQC reference, evidence.

4.2  How adaptation works technically
company.provider_type (Selection on res.company via settings) — the master switch.
Terminology: an nhs.terminology model maps logical labels ('person_affected', 'location_unit') to display strings per provider type. Views use a t-label helper; reports read the same table.
Category presets: nhs.incident.category seed data is tagged with provider_types (many2many-style char list); an install wizard activates only relevant branches.
Notification rules: nhs.notification.rule records map (provider_type, category, harm threshold) → required notification types (CQC / RIDDOR / LFPSE / safeguarding) with deadlines.
Public form variants: the website capture form renders category pickers and person labels from the active preset — a care home reporter sees 'Resident', a GP receptionist sees 'Patient'.

6.2  Naming decision
This module carries the 'NHS' brand, consistent with the rest of the portfolio (NHS Trust Management — Core / Operations & Compliance / Reports & Documents, UK Regions, ODS Sync). Rationale: (1) 'NHS' is the highest-value search keyword on the Apps Store for UK health-sector buyers and the brand the suite is already known by; (2) the strongest regulatory differentiators in this module — PSIRF, LFPSE, Duty of Candour — are NHS frameworks, so the name signals exactly the compliance depth buyers are looking for; (3) a single brand family compounds across listings. Technical name: odoo_nhs_incident_risk. Model prefix: nhs. (e.g. nhs.incident, nhs.risk). The module remains fully usable by non-NHS providers — care homes, dental practices, independent hospitals — and the listing copy states this explicitly: 'Built for the NHS. Ready for every CQC-registered provider — care homes, GP practices, hospices, clinics.' Architecture is unaffected: the module stays standalone with zero dependency on the trust suite.
7.  Detailed Feature List
7.1  Incident capture (the 'DIF1')
Public web form at /incident/report/<company-token> — no login required; token per company prevents spam across tenants.
Anonymous reporting toggle: reporter contact fields become optional; record flagged is_anonymous.
Progressive form: ~10 fields visible initially (what/when/where/who affected/description/immediate action), expanding panels for persons and witnesses.
Category picker: two-level hierarchical select driven by the provider-type preset.
Location picker: hierarchical (site → unit → room) from nhs.location.
Persons affected: repeatable sub-form — type (patient/resident/staff/visitor/contractor/other), name (optional if anonymous-subject), age band, harm observed.
File attachments (photos of hazard, etc.) up to configurable size.
Reporter receives a reference number (INC/2026/00421) and, if email given, confirmation + closure feedback emails.
Internal quick-create: licensed users can also log incidents directly from the backend with the same form embedded.
Draft auto-save on the public form (localStorage) so a ward sister interrupted mid-report doesn't lose the entry.
7.2  Triage & handling (the 'DIF2')
Triage queue: kanban grouped by status, badge counts, SLA colouring (new > 3 working days = red).
Validation actions: accept / reject (with reason, fed back to reporter) / mark duplicate (link to master incident).
Harm grading: NPSA grade + LFPSE physical & psychological harm; grading changes are tracked with reason.
Severity auto-rules: category + harm combination can auto-set response level and auto-create DoC / notification placeholders.
Person records upgrade: link an affected staff member to hr.employee (if hr installed — optional soft dependency), enabling RIDDOR pre-fill.
Linked records: incident ↔ incident (related/duplicate), incident ↔ risk (incident evidences a risk; 'create risk from incident' action).
Full chatter audit trail; every status change logged with actor and timestamp.
7.3  Investigation (PSIRF-aligned)
Response levels: none / swarm / aar / mdt_review / psii — selectable per incident, defaulted from category.
Investigation record: lead investigator, team members, terms of reference, chronology/timeline entries, findings.
Contributing factors: multi-select taxonomy based on the Yorkshire Contributory Factors Framework groups (patient factors, task, individual staff, team, environment, organisational, external).
Lessons learned + 'areas of good practice' fields (LFPSE 'good care' compatibility).
Report generation: investigation summary as PDF (QWeb) for sharing with the affected person under DoC.
Sign-off chain: investigator submits → quality lead approves → incident can close.
7.4  Statutory engines
Duty of Candour: auto-created at moderate+ harm; stages (verbal notification → written notification → findings shared) each with done date, evidence, responsible user; working-day deadline computation with UK bank-holiday awareness (editable holiday calendar).
RIDDOR: determination wizard (guided yes/no questions); outcome stored with category (death/specified/over-7-day/disease/dangerous occurrence), HSE report deadline computed, F2508 reference captured.
CQC notifications: rule-driven 'notification required' banner; tracker record with type, statutory basis, submitted date/by, CQC reference, attachment of submitted form.
LFPSE: event-type and harm fields aligned to LFPSE taxonomy; export wizard produces CSV/JSON batch of events ready for upload; submission log.
Safeguarding: flag + referral sub-record (LA contacted, date, reference) — full safeguarding module is roadmap.
7.5  Risk register
Risk record in cause→event→effect structure with guided placeholders.
5×5 scoring: consequence and likelihood pickers with full NPSA descriptor text in tooltips; auto-computed rating and RAG band (1–3 low, 4–6 moderate, 8–12 high, 15–25 extreme).
Three score sets: inherent, current, target — with progress indicator (current vs target gap).
Controls (one2many) and assurances (one2many, tagged 1st/2nd/3rd line) plus gap flags.
Tiered registers: nhs.risk.register records (e.g. 'Ward 7 local register', 'Corporate Risk Register', 'BAF'); escalation wizard moves a risk up/down a tier with logged rationale.
Review engine: review frequency auto-set from RAG band per policy table; next_review_date computed; overdue reviews appear in activity stream and dashboard; review log one2many.
Risk appetite: per-category appetite level; rating > appetite shows an 'outside appetite' badge.
Heatmap: 5×5 pivot-style matrix report showing risk counts per cell (v1 = pivot view + QWeb report; interactive widget is roadmap).
7.6  Actions (CAPA)
Single nhs.action model shared by incidents, investigations and risks (polymorphic via res_model/res_id + dedicated m2o convenience fields).
Fields: title, description, type (corrective/preventive/improvement), owner, due date, priority, status (open/in_progress/evidence_review/done/cancelled), completion evidence (text + attachments).
Overdue escalation: scheduled action emails owner at due-3 days, owner+manager at overdue, weekly digest to quality lead.
Effectiveness check: optional 'verify effectiveness after N days' flag re-opens a verification activity.
7.7  Dashboards & reporting
Incident dashboard: count by category/month, harm distribution, days-to-close, open by status, top locations — graph + pivot views with saved filters.
Risk dashboard: register heatmap, risks outside appetite, overdue reviews, escalations this quarter.
Statutory dashboard: open DoC clocks, RIDDOR submissions YTD, CQC notifications log, LFPSE export status.
Board pack PDF: monthly quality & safety summary (QWeb) — incident trends, new extreme risks, overdue actions.
All list/pivot data exportable to Excel natively.

11.  Workflows and State Machines
11.1  Incident lifecycle
            ┌──────────► rejected   (handler; reason mandatory; feedback emailed)
            │
new ───► triage ───► investigation ───► actions ───► pending_closure ───► closed
            │              ▲   (skipped when response_level='none':
            │              │    triage → actions directly)
            └──────────► duplicate  (linked to master; counts excluded from KPIs)
 
GATES:
  triage → investigation   : harm graded + category confirmed + response level set
  investigation → actions  : investigation state = approved
  actions → pending_closure: all nhs.action done/cancelled
  pending_closure → closed : DoC complete (if present) + CQC notifications resolved
                             + quality-lead group membership
State writes are guarded exactly like the trust suite: nhs.incident.write() raises UserError when 'state' is in vals without context nhs_workflow=True — every transition flows through the action methods, never raw writes. This is the audit-defensibility pattern CQC inspectors look for.
11.2  Duty of Candour clock
harm_grade set to moderate/severe/death
        │ (auto, _apply_harm_rules)
        ▼
nhs.duty.of.candour created — triggered_at = now
        │ written_deadline = +10 WORKING days (nhs.holiday calendar)
        ▼
verbal_done ──► written_done ──► findings_shared_done ──► state: complete
   │                │
   │                └─ 'Generate letter' renders QWeb DoC template
   └─ reminder activities at deadline-5d and deadline-2d; OVERDUE escalates
      to quality lead + red banner on incident and dashboard
11.3  Risk review cycle
active risk ──► next_review_date reached
        │  (daily cron)
        ▼
activity created for risk_owner_id ──► owner runs Review wizard
        │                                    │
        │            ┌───────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
        ▼            ▼                       ▼                    ▼
   no change     re-score (new C×L)     escalate (wizard)     close risk
        │            │                       │                    │
        └────────────┴── nhs.risk.review row logged; next_review_date reset
11.4  Notification rule evaluation
Rules fire at three moments: incident create, harm grade change, category change. Evaluation is idempotent — re-running never duplicates statutory placeholders, only adds newly-required ones and flags now-unnecessary ones for human dismissal (never auto-deletes a statutory record).

12.2  Record rules (highlights)
Company rule on every nhs.* model: [('company_id','in',company_ids)] — multi-home groups isolated per home where companies are used.
Reporter rule on nhs.incident: [('create_uid','=',user.id)] for group_hc_reporter — staff see their own reports and feedback, nothing else.
Location scoping for handlers: res.users.nhs_allowed_location_ids (m2m); rule ['|',('location_id','child_of',user.nhs_allowed_location_ids.ids),('handler_id','=',user.id)] — mirrors the ICB/LHB allow-list pattern from the trust suite.
Safeguarding rule: incidents with safeguarding_flag=True readable only by group_hc_safeguarding + quality leads — protects highly sensitive allegations.
Risk register tier rule: BAF and corporate registers writable only by quality leads; readable per register owner group.
No-delete rules on nhs.incident, nhs.duty.of.candour, nhs.riddor, nhs.cqc.notification — statutory records are never unlinked, only rejected/closed (archive).
12.3  Data protection stance
Minimum-necessary person data: age bands not DOB, initials encouraged, no clinical record duplication. The module is a safety-management system, not a care record — stated in README and listing.
Public form rate-limited per IP (controller-level) and token-gated per company; no data echoed back to unauthenticated users after submit except the reference number.
Field-level access: reporter contact details visible to handler+ groups only.
Retention helper: data-cleanup cron (disabled by default) anonymises reporter details N years after closure per local policy.

14.  Wizards
14.1  Triage wizard (nhs.triage.wizard)
Opened from the triage kanban/form. One screen: confirm category, confirm location, set harm grade + LFPSE harms, set response level (defaulted), decision accept/reject/duplicate (+reason/master).
On accept: applies grading, runs notification rules, creates investigation if response_level != none, advances state, assigns handler if empty.
Bulk mode: from list view selection, accept-only path for trivially-no-harm batches (e.g. near-miss clusters) — grading applied uniformly with confirmation.
14.2  RIDDOR determination wizard (nhs.riddor.wizard)
Guided question flow: Was anyone injured? Worker or non-worker? Fatal? Specified-injury checklist (fracture/amputation/sight loss/crush/burn/scalping/unconsciousness/enclosed-space)? Over-7-day incapacitation? Dangerous occurrence list? Disease diagnosis?
Outputs reportable + category + deadline; writes nhs.riddor with the full Q&A transcript in determination_log.
'Not reportable' is ALSO recorded — the negative determination with reasoning is the audit defence HSE inspectors ask for.
14.3  Risk escalation wizard (nhs.risk.escalate.wizard)
Fields: target register (domain: different tier), rationale (required), notify users (m2m, defaults to target register owners).
Writes register_id, logs nhs.risk.review row with decision='escalate', posts chatter on both registers, schedules acceptance activity for the receiving owner.
14.4  LFPSE export wizard (nhs.lfpse.export.wizard)
Date range + auto-selected incidents with lfpse_state='pending'; preview list; format csv/json.
Generates the mapped file (taxonomy version-tagged), creates nhs.lfpse.submission, attaches file, sets incidents to 'exported'.
14.5  Provider setup wizard (nhs.provider.setup.wizard)
First-run (launched from a banner until completed): pick provider_type → preview of category branches, terminology pack, notification rules that will activate → confirm.
Archives non-matching categories, activates the terminology pack, generates the public-form token and shows the QR code/poster PDF for staff rooms ('Scan to report an incident').
Re-runnable: switching provider type re-evaluates presets without deleting historical data.
